## THE 3-SECOND SURVIVAL THRESHOLD

Up to **50% of viewers drop off in the first 3 seconds.**
Reels with 3s hold rate above 60% outperform those below 40% by **5–10x in total reach.**

The brain needs an immediate visual pattern interrupt to stop automatic scrolling. In "low-attention state" (passive scroll), the brain is on autopilot — anything that breaks the expected flow stops it.

**What kills the hook instantly:**
- Any slow introduction ("Hey guys, today I'm going to show you...")
- Real test: "Hey, I'm Sarah..." averaged 18% 3s retention. "This mistake cost me $50K... and you're making it right now" → 67%.

## THE 4-PHASE RETENTION STRUCTURE

Map every second of the Reel to a deliberate attention function.

### Phase 1 — Pattern-Interrupt Hook (0–3 seconds)
Stop the scroll. See hook-library.md for all formulas.
Non-negotiable. If this fails, nothing else matters.

### Phase 2 — Problem Escalation (3–7 seconds)
Build emotional stakes. Don't just state the problem — make the viewer feel the cost of not paying attention.
> "And it's costing you more than you think..."

This bridges the hook to the value. Escalation is what keeps someone watching past curiosity.

### Phase 3 — High-Density Value Delivery (7–25 seconds)
**Value density** = ratio of useful information to total watch time. Every second must earn its place.

Rules for this phase:
- No filler. No repeated points. No "let me explain what I mean by that."
- Jump cuts: remove all dead air and pauses
- Visual change every **3–4 seconds** (swap angle, flash graphic, change b-roll, insert text card) to reset visual engagement
- Monotonous pacing = silent killer of retention (brain detects pattern → mentally checks out)

**Ideal pacing structure within Phase 3:**
```
Fast intro (5 cuts in 3s)
→ Dramatic pause with a question
→ Medium-paced development (cut every 2–3s)
→ Final acceleration (cuts every 0.5s)
→ Impactful close
```

**THE MIDPOINT INTERRUPT:** At the exact midpoint of the Reel, drop a mini-cliffhanger to reset attention:
> "But here's where it gets interesting..."
> "But the last step changes everything..."

Directly combats the natural mid-video attention dip.

### Phase 4 — The Loop Close (Final 2–3 seconds)
Structure the script so the final sentence flows directly into the first word of the video. No pause, no outro card, no "follow for more." Viewer seamlessly starts watching again without realizing it looped.

**Example:**
- Opening: "The number one reason your Reels aren't getting views is..."
- Closing: "...and that's exactly why — The number one reason your Reels aren't getting views is..."

**Advanced — The Hidden Challenge:**
> "I left one mistake in this Reel on purpose — did you catch it?"

Rewatch intent becomes deliberate. Viewer scans entire video again.

Real test: Standard ending = 8% rewatch rate. Hidden challenge ending = 34% rewatch rate, 580% reach growth.

**THE LOOP-CLOSE RISK (2026):** Audiences are now aware of the loop trap. A viewer who realizes they were tricked → irritation → aggressive swipe → "Not Interested" tap → strong negative signal to algorithm.

**Rule:** Use loops only when content genuinely rewards rewatching. Skip it when the content delivers full value in a single pass — use a strong CTA or emotional punctuation mark instead.

## OPTIMAL LENGTH BY GOAL

| Length | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 7–15s | Educational one-liners, strong visual hooks, quick tips | Highest completion rate, easiest to go viral |
| 15–30s | Instructional or story-driven content | Sweet spot for most creators |
| 30–60s | Deep-value explainers | Only if EVERY second earns its place |
| 60–90s | Narrative / tutorial with proven sustained retention | Highest engagement IF creator is skilled at retention |

**Golden rule:** Use the shortest length that delivers the message effectively. A 10s Reel at 80% retention beats a 60s Reel at 30% retention every time.

---

## THE COUNTER-TREND WARNING (2026)

Hyper-editing is now the baseline, not a differentiator. Jump cuts, kinetic text, and zoom-ins are used by everyone. Result: **dopamine fatigue.**

A counter-movement is growing: slower, unedited, authentic content that succeeds *because* it doesn't look like a calculated retention trap.

**When to use tight editing:** Educational, instructional, step-by-step content.
**When to use raw format:** Personal takes, confessions, behind-the-scenes, opinions.

The most effective 2026 strategy is **variable format** — alternate between both.

## RETENTION SIGNALS TO MONITOR (Post-Publish)

Check these in Insights within 24–72 hours:
- **Completion rate** → did the idea hold attention throughout?
- **Skip rate** → high skip rate in first 3s = death sentence; rewrite the hook
- **Rewatch rate** → measures whether loop close or hidden challenge worked
- **Retention curve** → shows exactly where viewers dropped off; fix that moment in future Reels
